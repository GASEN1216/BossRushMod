## Black Market Duck Cup

### What Is It?

You are not a fighter here. You are a manager.

Sign two contenders, read the odds, call exactly one order, and let them fight all six matches for you.

You never step into the ring. You spend the whole season in the stands with nothing but a bell. How it goes is half about who you signed and half about the handful of decisions you make before each opening whistle.

Six matches per season. Match 6 is the final. There is no match 7.

### Getting In

- Interact with **the boat at the base dock**. Alongside "Boss Rush" there is a second option: **Black Market Duck Cup**.
- Costs one **BossRush Ticket**, exactly like a normal BossRush run. It is charged when you confirm on the map selection page, not before.
- **No mode-specific item is required**, and you do not have to strip your gear.
- **Only DEMO Ultimate Challenge for now.** The mode needs a map to define a ring, stands, a
  staging area and an exit, and DEMO is the only one audited so far. The map screen only lists
  what works, so you cannot pick wrong - there just isn't a choice to make.
- You cannot enter while another mode is running (Standard BossRush, From Scratch, Faction War, Blood Hunt, Fate Echo and Zombie Mode all count).
- If entry is turned away anyway — content not ready, unsuitable venue, a clash with another mode — **your ticket comes back**, along with the reason.

Getting in does not start a fight. First comes the tryout.

### The Tryout: Sign Two, Lose Three

Candidates are dealt once per season: **five fighters, one of each role** — Assault, Ranged, Tank, Sustain, and Finisher. Each card lists the role, the temperament, a quirk, and a rumor about them.

- The display order is fixed. **Closing the page and reopening it does not reroll anything**, so do not bother trying.
- Sign your **starter** first, then your **relay**.
- The three you pass on immediately draw a destination card each:
  - **Return Ticket** — comes back as the enemy core in match 5
  - **Waitlist** — may show up in the transfer window after match 2, giving you one more shot at them
  - **Torn Up** — removed for the rest of the season; nobody gets them

So while you are picking, spend a second on the other question too: which of these three do you least want to meet in match 5?

### How a Season Runs

Six matches, all on the same rhythm:

**Brief → Loadout → Odds → Lock In → Entry → Bell → Report**

- A match lasts at most **180 seconds**. If you have not won by then, you lose. There is no extra time.
- The opposition escalates every match. The first two cap at two enemies on the field at once; from match 3 the cap is three.
- A **transfer window** opens after the settlement of **match 2** and **match 4**.
- After match 6, your champion goes into the **Hall of Fame**.

#### Transfer Windows

Only two all season, and **there is no refreshing**. You get what you get, and sometimes you get nothing.

- **After match 2** — your Waitlist fighter may come knocking.
- **After match 4** — a special enemy you just beat may be willing to sign.
- Accepting one bumps your **relay**. The fighter you dropped is released on the spot and **cannot be re-signed this season**.
- **You can never sell your starter.** The only way the relay is promoted is if the starter retires while the relay is still standing.

### Loadouts and Reward Kits

Loadouts use gear the mode hands out. It has **nothing to do with your warehouse** and consumes none of your own supplies.

Each fighter carries up to **4 pieces** across five slots — armor, helmet, primary weapon, secondary weapon, and melee weapon — one piece per slot.

Win a match and you are offered one to three **reward kits**. Pick one to unlock it for the rest of the season. How many you are offered depends on how much your stake won that match.

### Reading the Board and Betting

The enemy plan is frozen **before** you start building your loadout. What you see on the board is final — nothing you equip makes them swap anyone in — so build straight against it.

The brief discloses their **head count range**, **primary role**, **entry pace**, **ring condition**, and any **known high-threat core**. The rest you have to guess at.

#### Free Scouting: One Look Per Match

The bottom of the brief gives you **one free scout**, once per match. Pick one of four:

- **Hidden Quirk** — reveals the enemy core's quirk and temperament
- **Current Injuries** — reveals how many of them are carrying wounds
- **Members & Order** — reveals how many arrive in each wave (counts only, not who)
- **Secondary Gear** — reveals the enemy core's extra capability tags

Which one you want depends on how you plan to bet: unsure whether to go big, check injuries; worried one particular core will turn the match, check the quirk. **The reveal folds into the public summary, so it moves the odds too** — showing that they're wounded shifts the board your way.

Once used, that row shows which one you picked and the buttons are gone.

#### Where the Odds Come From

The odds are a straight conversion of the public score gap, from x1 to x5:

- Gap of **20 or more** → **x1**, heavy favorite
- Gap of **5 to 19** → **x2**, slight favorite
- Gap of **-9 to 4** → **x3**, even money
- Gap of **-24 to -10** → **x4**, underdog
- Gap of **-25 or less** → **x5**, long shot

**How much you bet does not move the odds.** A small probing bet will not shorten the price.

#### Virtual Credits

You start each season with **6 credits**, capped at 30. Each match you may stake **0 to 2**, never more than your balance, and **staking nothing is always allowed**.

- A win pays **stake × (1 + odds)**
- Every 2 credits of net profit adds one more reward kit candidate that match, up to 3
- A loss deducts the stake

One line worth memorizing: **a full 2-credit stake never loses money on a win, at any price.** If you want reward kits, do not be shy. Read it the other way and the math still holds: 6 starting credits absorb exactly three full-stake misses.

[tip] A 1-credit stake only matches "no stake" at x1, where both give a single candidate. That is the conservative option, left in on purpose.

### The Bell: The One Thing You Actually Do

You get exactly one active input all match: **ring the bell**.

- **Before the match**, lock in one order.
- **During the match you may ring once**, and the order runs for **6 seconds**.
- Ringing **does not pause the fight and does not open a menu**. The clock keeps running.

Eight common orders: **Steady / Press / Center / Spread / Finish / Hold / Guard / All In**.

Five more are signature orders, available only while the fighter who owns them is in the ring: **Weak Point / Anchor / Last Mag / Together / Hand Off**.

A few situations make the bell do nothing, and those **do not consume your use** — you can ring again once things change: nobody on the field can take the order, the signature order's owner is not in the ring, the order needs the relay fighter to enter first, or the enemy count does not meet its condition. The screen tells you which one it was.

[tip] Six seconds is short and you only get one. Do not burn it at the opening whistle. Save it for the moment the match actually turns: reinforcements arriving, the enemy core showing itself, or your fighter starting to fold.

### Fighters Get Hurt, and Fighters Retire

The two fighters you signed are a resource you can run out of.

- **Going down once** leaves a fighter **injured**.
- An **injured fighter who enters again and goes down again retires for the season** and cannot be used any more.
- An injured fighter who **never sets foot in the ring** during a match **recovers** afterwards.

That last line matters: benching an injured fighter genuinely pays off, because they really do heal. Pushing them out for one more match can cost you them permanently. The match report says plainly who was **Fully Rested** and who **Retired for the Season**.

If your starter retires, the relay is promoted. **If both contracted fighters retire, the season ends right there** — even in match 3.

### Real Stakes (Read This Before You Bet)

[warn] This mode lets you stake real warehouse items. A loss confiscates them permanently, and your only copy of a piece of gear is not exempt.

This is the **only** place in the whole BossRush mod that touches your real property. That is why the warning line at the top of the entry page cannot be dismissed.

- **Entirely optional.** Nothing is staked by default. You can play a full six-match season without ever staking an item, and the virtual credit track is unaffected.
- **At most 3 items per match.**
- On a loss, everything you staked is **gone permanently**. No partial refunds, no mercy for the one and only copy of a piece of gear.
- On a win, **your stake comes back in full**, plus extra items of the same quality equal to the raw odds multiplier: x3 pays 3 items, x5 pays 5. The quality matches the **highest-quality** item you put up.

Real stakes and virtual credits are **two parallel bets**. Neither converts into the other and neither affects the other.

[warn] Before you stake, ask yourself one question: if these items were gone tomorrow, would I still want to play this save? Answer it first, then click.

### The Hall of Fame: 32 Seats, and You Can Be Pushed Out

Win the sixth match and your champion is inscribed in the **Hall of Fame**.

- The Hall **persists across seasons** and holds exactly **32 seats**. When the thirty-third arrives, **the oldest record is pushed out**.
- Each record carries the champion's name, archetype and temperament, signature order, that season's **best odds win** and **best credit win**, and how many scars they carried.
- A champion with an anomaly is marked apart in the list.
- The season wrap-up page shows you the current Hall directly, so you can see how many seats are left and who is about to be pushed out.

Being pushed out is not a failure. It is what the rule was always going to do — names not lasting is the normal state of this place.

### Questions You Might Have

- **Can I fight myself?** No. You are the manager here, and you stay in the stands the whole way.
- **Can I bring my Wild Seed companions?** No. You never enter the ring, so there is nothing for them to follow.
- **Are there mutation affixes?** No. This mode applies no mutation affixes and fires no in-run random events. What the board says is what walks into the ring.
- **Do kills here count toward the Duck King Codex?** No. Your fighter landed the blow, not you.
- **Can I reroll the candidates?** No. Closing and reopening the page changes nothing. Those five are the five.
- **What happens after a season?** The season closes and your champion is recorded in the **Hall of Fame**. The Hall keeps only **32 places** — the 33rd entry pushes out the oldest one.
- **Does a technical failure count as a loss?** No. The match restarts from the same brief and no defeat is recorded.

[tip] The real work in this mode happens before the whistle: who you signed, which order you locked, and whether you dared to stake the full two credits. Once the bell rings, the rest is up to them.
